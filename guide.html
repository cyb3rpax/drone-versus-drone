How drones work

A delivery drone is a quadcopter: four motors, four propellers, a battery and a small computer that makes thousands of tiny corrections every second. There are no flaps or rudder — every move comes from changing how fast each motor spins.

The best way to understand a drone is to build one. The Courier Kit is our own hand-sized design: two tinted acrylic decks, four tiny brushless motors in printed pods, ring guards, a round controller board, a 2S battery, and a gamepad remote. It has the same parts as a big delivery drone, small enough to fit in your hand.

🔷 Two acrylic decks

Laser-cut tinted acrylic: a cyan lower deck with four tapered arms, and a smaller pink canopy on brass standoffs. The electronics sit between them, and you can see the LED ring glowing through the top.

🌀 Brushless motors + 3-blade props

Four tiny outrunner motors clamp into printed pods at the end of each arm. Cyan props spin clockwise and pink ones counter-clockwise, so the twisting forces cancel out.

🧠 Courier Core

A round controller board with the gyroscope and barometer that keep it level, four motor drivers, a radio, a tiny OLED readout and a USB-C port for code and charging. The motor leads plug straight into it.

🔋 2S LiPo battery

7.4 V and 450 mAh, in a printed cradle under the lower deck with a velcro strap. Good for a few minutes of flying, which is why the game makes you recharge at Harmony.

🛡 Ring guards

A printed ring around each propeller on three spokes, so a bump into a wall spins you instead of snapping a blade.

🎮 Gamepad remote

Two thumbsticks, a little status screen and a radio antenna. The left stick is throttle and turn, the right stick tilts the drone to fly forward, back and sideways.

Scaling it up: a full-size delivery drone

A real delivery drone works the same way. It just has bigger brushless motors, speed controllers, GPS and a payload hook. Here it is from directly above, with what every part does.

🌀

Lift

Each propeller is a spinning wing. It pushes air down, and the air pushes the drone up. When all four together push harder than gravity pulls, the drone climbs.

🔄

Why two spin backwards

A spinning prop tries to twist the drone the opposite way. So diagonal pairs spin opposite directions (two clockwise, two counter-clockwise) and the twists cancel out.

🧠

The flight controller

A gyro and accelerometer act like your inner ear. The flight controller reads them thousands of times a second and adjusts each motor so the drone stays level, even in a gust.

🔋

Battery = range

Lithium-polymer (LiPo) batteries are light and powerful, but hovering eats energy fast. Most delivery drones fly 15–40 minutes, which is why the game makes you recharge at Harmony.

🛰

GPS + compass

GPS says where the drone is and the compass says which way it faces. Together they let it hold its position, fly a route, and come home by itself if the radio link drops.

📦

Carrying a package

The payload hangs under the centre so it doesn’t tip the drone. Heavier cargo means more thrust just to hover, so there’s less speed and battery left over. In the game, gold bullion flies slower than mail.

How the game works — the full schematic

Every screen and every step, in order. Numbered boxes are the normal loop. Red is what happens when things go wrong. The dashed box is the server that keeps your account safe.

Your real route

Your address is looked up once and turned into a straight-line distance to Harmony Montessori in Tiburon. The world is stretched to match, so the drone flies at the same speed on every route. Longer routes just take longer.

Coins in, coins out

Every round trip pays 🪙100 (custom missions pay more for tougher conditions). You spend coins in the hangar on drones, paint and crash effects, and at home on furniture and floors.

Crashes cost you

Hit something too fast and a part breaks. Which part depends on how you hit it. You carry the drone home, set it on the repair desk, buy the part, and screw it on yourself.

Nothing overlaps

The town, the forest, your rooftop and every piece of furniture in your room are placed on a reserved grid. Two things can never sit in the same spot, and your path to the desk and stairs always stays clear.

Repairs

Drag each screw onto the glowing mount hole on your drone, then turn it clockwise: ⬆➡⬇⬅ — four quarter-turns per screw, four screws per part.

The rules of the sky

  1. One battery per leg. It drains the whole flight and covers only about half the round trip. Watch the range estimate against the miles to go.
  2. Recharge on pads only. Your home pad and Harmony's pad are your lifelines. Run dry over open water and you ditch at sea.
  3. The round trip pays. Reaching Harmony isn't enough — land back home to bank your 🪙100.
  4. Grab the field battery. One floats along your route each run. Fly through it for an instant free full charge.
  5. Terrain wins every argument. Trees, houses, apartment towers, hills and rocks are all solid. Gentle taps bounce; fast hits break parts.
  6. Birds match your altitude. You can't climb over a flock — steer around or thread the formation.
  7. Crashes send you home. The screen tells you how you died. Then you carry the wreck home and set it on your repair desk.
  8. You do the repairs. Order the part at the desk (on credit if you're broke), then screw it on yourself.

What breaks, and when

Damage depends on how you crash — eleven parts, each with its own mount point on the airframe.
⬇️

Belly-flop the ground

Landing gear, battery cell, GPS antenna or an arm strut.

➡️

Nose-first into something

Camera gimbal, nav lights, frame plates — or the flight computer, the priciest part in the game.

↔️

Side-swipe

Rotor assembly, arm strut or motor mount — the spinning props clip first.

🐦

Bird strike

Rotors, prop guards, motor mount or nav lights.

💡 Pro tip: fly high over open water to save battery, then drop low only when you need to thread the islands. Climbing costs power — but so does a repair bill.
